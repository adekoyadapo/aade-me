# Cloud Computing Over 10 Years: A 2015-2025 Review

A decade is long enough to completely transform an industry. Looking back at cloud computing from 2015 to 2025, the shift from "risky experiment" to "default assumption" is pretty remarkable.

## 2015: Multi-Cloud Wasn't a Buzzword Yet

In 2015, businesses started experimenting with using more than one cloud provider. This was actually controversial at the time. The conventional wisdom was to pick a vendor and commit. Splitting across providers seemed unnecessarily complicated.

Back then, only a minority of corporate data lived in the cloud. The rest was sitting in on-premise data centers with IT teams nervously protecting their physical servers like dragon hoards.

## The 2020s Shift

Cloud providers stopped being just infrastructure and started offering services that actually mattered: AI, machine learning, serverless, automation. The cloud became a platform for building things instead of just a place to host them.

OpenAI's GPT-4 integrated with cloud platforms in 2023 and suddenly every startup could access advanced AI without building a supercomputer. That changed the game entirely.

## The Numbers

By 2025, most organisations kept a large share of their data in the cloud. That's not incremental growth. That's a fundamental shift in how businesses operate.

[Gartner forecast](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-11-19-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-public-cloud-end-user-spending-to-total-723-billion-dollars-in-2025) worldwide public cloud end-user spending of $723.4 billion in 2025, up from $595.7 billion in 2024. That's 21.5% growth in a single year, which is wild for a market this size.

## What's Actually Happening

Many organizations now run hybrid cloud or multi-cloud. Gartner predicts that [90% of organizations will adopt a hybrid cloud approach through 2027](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-11-19-gartner-forecasts-worldwide-public-cloud-end-user-spending-to-total-723-billion-dollars-in-2025).

The reason is simple: no single provider is best at everything. AWS for compute, GCP for AI, Azure for enterprise integration. Pick the right tool for each job instead of forcing everything into one vendor's ecosystem.

Most organizations now use or experiment with generative AI services. What was science fiction in 2015 is now a line item in procurement.

Edge computing is maturing too: processing data closer to where it's generated reduces latency for IoT, autonomous vehicles, and real-time applications.

## Market Share

As of Q3 2025, [market-tracker figures reported by TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/news/The-big-three-grab-two-thirds-of-107B-cloud-market-in-Q3/857425/) puts the big three at 63% of global cloud infrastructure spending:
- AWS: 29% (still the leader)
- Azure: 20% (the enterprise choice)
- Google Cloud: 13% (the AI specialist)

## What Changed

In 2015, the question was "should we move to the cloud?"

In 2025, the question is "how do we optimize our cloud strategy?"

That shift from "if" to "how" means the industry has matured. The cloud isn't the future anymore. It's infrastructure. The interesting questions are about optimization, cost management, and multi-cloud orchestration.

Serverless computing went from simple functions to full applications. Sustainability became a competitive differentiator: cloud providers compete on carbon neutrality now. Security evolved from an afterthought to a core selling point.

## Looking Forward

The next decade will be about making all this complexity manageable. Better tooling, better observability, better cost controls. The technology works. Now we need to figure out how to operate it without burning out our teams or our budgets.
